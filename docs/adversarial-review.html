Executive Summary — The Five Blockers

Everything below was verified against the paper source (paper/cikm/), the generated tables, and the underlying result CSVs (results/stats-v3/, results/main-v2-judged.csv, results/main-v3-judged-pinned.csv). Where a number in this review is stated as fact, it was recomputed from the repository data, not taken on faith from the paper.

B1 🔴 The flagship C3 statistic does not measure what the text says it measures. LEGITIMATE
§6.3 (sec:c3) lines 91–93; Table 3 caption; Abstract C3

§6.3 claims: "the same judge reading the same question and pipeline output changes verdict on 41% of items when only the retrieval embedding changes." The pipeline output is not the same. Recomputation on the 300 pinned tuples: 283/300 (94%) have different cited-ID sets between v2 and v3 — the embedder swap changes retrieval, which changes the generated answer, which the judge then correctly judges as a different item. The verdict flips (123/300 for GPT-5.4 = 41% ✓; 78/300 for GPT-4.1 = 26% ✓) conflate output drift with judge instability. As written, C3's "first quantification of single-judge fragility in a controlled paired design" is refutable from the paper's own artifacts: the design is not controlled in the variable that matters.

Fix: (a) Reframe C3 as "faithfulness verdicts are embedder-conditional because the full pipeline state is" — which the Discussion (§7, "Implications") already states correctly; make §6.3 and the abstract match the Discussion, not vice versa. (b) The salvageable judge-specific evidence is the differential: on the identical set of changed items, GPT-5.4 flips 41% vs GPT-4.1's 26% — same inputs, 15 pp more instability is attributable to the judge. Lead with that. (c) Ideal (2–3 h, ~$3): re-judge the identical v2 answers twice with GPT-5.4 to establish the same-input flip-rate baseline (test–retest); report it next to the cross-embedder rate.
B2 🔴 C2b's cross-corpus leg rests on the judge configuration C3 discredits — and the Discussion concedes it while the Abstract claims it. LEGITIMATE
Abstract (C2b); §5 "Faithfulness judging"; §6.2; §7 Threats ("cross-corpus replication of C1 and C2b is left for the journal extension"); §2 ("we use MuSiQue for cross-corpus replication of C2a only")

The MuSiQue faithfulness numbers (42%→54%→58%) are single-judged by GPT-5.4 — the judge the paper itself shows to be the least stable (self-κ 0.137). So the headline "corpus-conditional consequence" contrast compares a two-judge DO-178C protocol against a one-fragile-judge MuSiQue protocol. Worse, the paper says this three different ways: the Abstract asserts C2b cross-corpus as a finding; Related Work says MuSiQue replicates C2a only; Threats defers C2b cross-corpus replication to a journal extension. A reviewer quoting these three sentences side by side writes the rejection paragraph for you.

Fix: Cheapest real fix (~$5, one evening): run GPT-4.1 over the same 600 MuSiQue rows and report both judges — if the rise holds under the stable judge, C2b becomes solid. Otherwise: downgrade the abstract to "single-judge evidence suggests the consequence reverses on Wikipedia chains" and make the three scope sentences agree.
B3 🔴 Corpus provenance story contradicts the public record attached to this project. LEGITIMATE — integrity-level, worse than any reviewer comment
§99 GenAI Disclosure (a); §7 Threats (External); vs. repo README, HF dataset card meftun/aerosys-requirements, Kaggle card

The paper states the corpus is "derived from proprietary aerospace certification artifacts… LLM-anonymized for release because the raw source artifacts are subject to IP and export-control restrictions." The project's own README, HANDOFF, and the publicly published dataset cards (Hugging Face + Kaggle, CC BY 4.0) describe the same 1,132-requirement corpus as a synthetic corpus, and the earlier disclosure draft (docs/plan.md §6) says GPT-5.4 generated it from a hand-authored taxonomy. Both stories cannot be true. If the paper's provenance claim is inaccurate, that is a research-integrity problem, not a reviewing problem. Additionally: (a) the public dataset is trivially findable (unique 1,132-requirement count, module names), and its slug carries an author handle — a de-anonymization vector during review; (b) "export-controlled source rewritten by an LLM and released CC BY 4.0" is self-undermining — if export control applied, an LLM paraphrase does not obviously discharge it, and a reviewer with aerospace exposure will say so; (c) module count is inconsistent (paper §4.2: 32 modules; README: 30).

Fix: Decide the single true provenance story and make paper, GenAI disclosure, dataset cards, and README say the same thing. If the corpus is synthetic-from-taxonomy, say exactly that (it is defensible — cite the anonymized-release precedent and keep the DO-178C-style framing); delete the export-control sentence entirely. This must be resolved before camera-ready or any arXiv posting.
B4 🔴 The pipeline named "GraphRAG" is not GraphRAG. LEGITIMATE
§1 (cites Edge et al. as "Microsoft GraphRAG"); §4.1(iii) ("vector seeds + up-to-2-hop typed-graph walk via Cypher, single synthesis")

The paper's headline pathology ("GraphRAG over-cites… architecturally universal") is measured on a custom vector-seed + 2-hop typed-edge Cypher walk. Edge et al.'s GraphRAG builds entity graphs with community detection and hierarchical summarization — a different architecture with different citation behavior. The intro invokes Microsoft GraphRAG and prior GraphRAG-vs-RAG literature, then evaluates a homegrown graph-walk retriever under the same name. An expert reviewer's cheapest kill-shot: "the universal pathology is a pathology of your graph walk; no published GraphRAG system was evaluated."

Fix: One honest sentence in §4.1 — "our graph pipeline is a typed-edge local-walk retriever in the GraphRAG family (vector seeds + bounded typed-edge expansion), not Edge et al.'s community-summarization system; we use 'GraphRAG' as the family label" — plus soften "architecturally universal" to "universal across our settings for typed-edge expansion retrievers." Better: rename the pipeline graph-walk throughout (mechanical, ~30 min).
B5 🔴 Compliance pair: main text spills past page 4, and the abstract's run count contradicts Table 1. LEGITIMATE
Compiled PDF p.5 (conclusion overrun above "GENAI USAGE DISCLOSURE"); Abstract ("4,440 main-matrix runs") vs Table 1 caption ("v2 main … 4,440 runs; v3 main … 4,440 runs")

(a) In the current build, the last two lines of the Conclusion land on page 5. CIKM's 4-page limit excludes references and the GenAI disclosure — not conclusion text. As compiled, this is a desk-reject-checkable violation. (b) The design is 4,440 runs per embedder (2 × 4,440 = 8,880 main-matrix runs) plus 600 MuSiQue runs; the abstract and intro say "4,440 main-matrix runs." The paper under-counts its own experiment and contradicts its own table caption — an easy credibility ding for a careful reviewer and a wrong number in every future citation of the paper.

Fix: Trim three lines (the TikZ figure or Related Work para 1 gives them up painlessly); change to "2×4,440 main-matrix runs (both embedders)". 20 minutes total.

1 seed (vs 3), Azure embedder only, 3 of 5 pipelines, single judge, and a graph built from gold supporting-paragraph chains (REFERENCES edges between consecutive gold paragraphs). The last is the sharp one: the MuSiQue graph encodes gold-adjacency — graph-walk retrieval there has oracle-flavored structure that the DO-178C graph does not. GraphRAG winning MuSiQue 2/3+-hop may partially reflect that construction. The paper does not discuss it.

Fix: one Threats sentence naming the gold-derived-edges caveat; ideally also report graph-walk hit-rate on gold vs non-gold edges (computable from existing traces).
🟢 Otherwise clean: shared generator/prompt/vector store across pipelines, uniform no-reranker policy, pinned judged tuples, locked CSVs, deterministic MuSiQue subgraph seed. No train/test contamination surface exists for the main F1 claims (no learned components except V2, handled above). Pass 12 — Statistical Validity & Variance Reporting

🔴 The C2b faithfulness cells carry no uncertainty and are individually within noise. LEGITIMATE Table 2 bottom block; §6.2

DO-178C judged cells are ~19–22 items each (300 tuples ÷ 5 pipelines ÷ 3 strata). 74% vs 40% at n≈20 per cell: Fisher exact p ≈ 0.06 — the headline collapse is not significant cell-to-cell, and no interval or test is reported anywhere in the faithfulness table. MuSiQue cells (~67) fare little better (42% vs 58%, p ≈ 0.09). The paper that ships BCa + Holm + Cliff's δ for F1 ships raw percentages for faithfulness — reviewers will read the asymmetry as convenient. This is the easiest legitimate reject-paragraph available after B1/B2.

Pass 13 — Efficiency, Compute & Cost

The paper makes no efficiency claims, so nothing is unmeasured-but-asserted — clean. Pass 14 — Generalization & Robustness

Narrowest honest scope statement the results support: "For one typed-edge synthetic regulated corpus and one Wikipedia multi-hop benchmark, under two embedders, two same-vendor judges, and one graph-walk implementation, over-citation of the walk retriever persisted across all settings while its faithfulness consequence and per-stratum winners varied by corpus."

What the abstract currently implies: architecture-family universality ("architecturally universal", "minimum bar for trustworthy RAG architecture claims"). Gap drivers, in order: single graph implementation (B4) > two embedders → "embedder-robust" (n=2, and 384d→1536d is also a dimensionality/quality confound, not a pure embedder identity swap) > generated query distribution (C4 circularity) > same-vendor judges. The irony a smart reviewer will enjoy: a paper whose thesis is "single-setting claims don't transfer" generalizes its own claims from 2-point axes. Pre-empt with one self-aware sentence in §7 — it converts the attack into a demonstrated understanding of the paper's own logic. 🟡

R1 — Domain expert (RAG evaluation / IR) · Weak Reject · confidence 4/5

Summary. The paper evaluates five RAG pipelines over a typed-edge requirements corpus and a MuSiQue subset, varying embedder and judge, and claims (i) universal over-citation for GraphRAG with corpus-conditional faithfulness consequences, (ii) corpus-conditional per-stratum winners, and (iii) embedder-fragility of single-judge faithfulness.

Strengths. Ambitious factorial design for a short paper; strong statistical protocol for citation F1 (BCa, Holm, Cliff's δ, ties reported as ties); the mechanism/consequence factorization of the GraphRAG debate is a genuinely useful framing; pinned-tuple cross-embedder judging is a clever design.

Weaknesses. (1) §6.3 claims the judge flips verdicts on "the same question and pipeline output"; under an embedder swap the retrieved context and generated answer necessarily differ, so self-κ conflates output drift with judge instability — the central C3 claim is not established by the design. (2) The MuSiQue faithfulness leg of C2b uses a single judge (GPT-5.4) that the paper itself shows to be unstable; §7 concedes C2b cross-corpus replication is future work while the abstract asserts it. (3) The evaluated "GraphRAG" is a custom 2-hop typed-edge walk, not Edge et al.'s system; family-level claims ("architecturally universal") from one implementation are not supported. (4) Faithfulness percentages come from ~20-item cells with no intervals or tests. (5) Retrieval top-k and walk parameters are unreported.

Justification. The measurement infrastructure is real and the factorization is novel, but the two headline claims (C2b cross-corpus, C3) exceed what the design establishes, and the naming issue undermines the universality claim. I would raise to weak accept if C3 were reframed as embedder-conditioning of the full pipeline state with the judge-differential argument, and C2b's MuSiQue leg were dual-judged or downgraded.
