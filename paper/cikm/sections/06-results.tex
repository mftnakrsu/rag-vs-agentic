\subsection{C1: Winners are Corpus-Conditional, Embedder- and Seed-Robust}
\label{sec:c1}
Table~\ref{tab:dominance} reports per-stratum $F_1$ across three settings.
On DO-178C the pattern repeats under both embedders: vanilla and
GraphRAG are statistically tied on 1-hop and 2-hop (Wilcoxon
$p{=}0.12/0.65$ local, negligible $\delta$), the agentic loop costs
0.19--0.30 $F_1$ on those strata, and on 3+-hop the ordering inverts --
vanilla drops to last and the graph-aided pipelines lead (local
embedder: agentic-graph 0.219 vs.\ vanilla 0.089, Holm
$p{<}10^{-6}$; under the Azure embedder the same ordering holds but no
3+-hop pair passes the joint significance criterion). A third embedder
from a third vendor, BAAI \texttt{bge-m3} (1024d), reproduces it again
(Table~\ref{tab:embedders}): vanilla is last at 3+-hop under all three
(0.089 / 0.195 / 0.114) while a graph-aided pipeline leads under all
three, and under bge-m3 both graph arms beat vanilla at 3+-hop
($+0.100$ and $+0.091$, Holm $p{=}2.1{\times}10^{-4}$ and
$1.4{\times}10^{-3}$) while the 1-hop and 2-hop contrasts stay
non-significant with negligible $\delta$. On MuSiQue
(three-pipeline field) GraphRAG wins every stratum outright
($F_1{=}0.841/0.632/0.463$; vs.\ vanilla, Wilcoxon
$p{\leq}5{\times}10^{-4}$, $\delta{=}0.27$--$0.43$). Because the
MuSiQue graph's \textit{REFERENCES} edges connect only gold supporting
paragraphs, we re-ran the GraphRAG arm on a control graph with
consecutive-distractor edges added (context precision falls
$0.23{\to}0.14$; the walk cap is always reached). GraphRAG loses only
$0.02/0.05/0.09$ $F_1$ per stratum and still beats vanilla everywhere
($+0.08/+0.16/+0.10$, $p{\leq}0.014$): the MuSiQue win is not an
artifact of gold-only edges. Seed variance is small against these
effects: across the three seeds, per-pipeline mean $F_1$ moves by at
most $0.031$ (max SD $0.016$), and every ordering we report as
significant holds independently in all three seeds; the only rank
swaps are between vanilla and GraphRAG on 1--2-hop, the pair we report
as tied. Which architecture wins is thus corpus- and
stratum-conditional, but stable under embedder swap and reseeding.

\subsection{C2: The Walk Floods the Context, the Synthesizer Filters It}
\label{sec:c2}
\noindent Table~\ref{tab:pathology} reports the same measurement in
six settings: three embedders, three corpora, and two traversals.
GraphRAG's walk fills 11--15 context slots at context precision
0.12--0.23 -- four to five of every six retrieved chunks are off-gold.
The synthesizer then cites only 2.5--5.0 IDs per answer, at citation
precision 0.48--0.98: a 2.9--4.2$\times$ precision enrichment over its
own context, in every setting. GraphRAG's $F_1$ edge is therefore a
\emph{recall} effect: the walk surfaces gold chunks dense retrieval
misses, and the synthesizer declines to cite most of the noise that
rides along. Evaluations that score the retrieved set as the
attribution set -- as some GraphRAG comparisons do -- measure the
flooding and miss the filtering, and invert the ranking: by context
precision GraphRAG is the worst pipeline in every setting; by answer
citations it is the best or tied-best.

Two controls show the split is not an artifact of our own design
choices. First, \emph{traversal}: replacing the bounded 2-hop walk with
personalized PageRank over the same graph -- unbounded in radius and
ranked by random-walk mass rather than hop distance, with embedder,
seeds and context cap held fixed -- changes almost nothing (context
precision $0.131$ vs.\ $0.129$; enrichment $3.9\times$ vs.\
$3.8\times$; citation $F_1$ $+0.016$ at Cliff's $\delta{=}0.036$, which
our joint criterion rejects as negligible). Second, \emph{mitigation}:
neither obvious fix helps. Reranking the walked neighbours by query
similarity and keeping the top five leaves everything where it was
(context precision $0.227{\to}0.232$, citation $F_1$ $+0.010$,
$p{=}0.34$), while tightening the traversal budget actively hurts
(walk $30{\to}10$, context $15{\to}8$: citation $F_1$ $-0.126$,
$p{=}2.6{\times}10^{-11}$, and context precision \emph{falls} to
$0.194$). This is what C2 predicts: the synthesizer already performs
the filtering, so filtering again upstream is redundant, and cutting
the budget only removes the recall the advantage rests on.

\subsection{C3: The Decline Follows Context Expansion, Not the Corpus}
\label{sec:c3}
An earlier version of this analysis reported the faithfulness decline as
\emph{corpus-conditional}: present on DO-178C, absent on Wikipedia
chains. Judging the full matrix rather than 300-row subsets, and
bringing the MuSiQue arm up to comparable power, withdraws that claim.

The MuSiQue arm had 67 rows per stratum, since 200 queries split three
ways. Its observed 1-hop to 3+-hop drop was $9.2$\,pp -- larger than the
$4.7$\,pp drop that reaches significance on DO-178C -- but detecting it
at 80\% power needs about 210 rows per cell. Re-running that arm at
three seeds ($n{=}600$, $\approx$200 per cell, judged in the same window
by the same judge) makes the decline significant there too:
$0.935/0.861/0.828$, Cochran--Armitage $p{=}1.2{\times}10^{-3}$, a
$10.7$\,pp drop at $p{=}9.1{\times}10^{-4}$, comparable to the
$11.9$\,pp we measure on DO-178C under the Azure embedder. The apparent
corpus-conditionality was an artifact of an underpowered cell.

What does separate pipelines is whether they expand the context at all.
Judging all 4{,}440 rows per embedder (per-stratum $n\approx300$ rather
than $\approx33$), \emph{vanilla -- the only pipeline that retrieves a
fixed top-5 and expands nothing -- never declines}: $p{=}0.16$, $0.99$
and $0.76$ under the three embedders. GraphRAG declines under e5-small
($p{=}0.039$) and strongly under 3-small ($p{=}1.4{\times}10^{-6}$),
though only marginally under bge-m3 ($p{=}0.065$); the agentic arms
decline in four of six embedder--pipeline combinations. We therefore
state the weaker claim the data supports: the non-expanding baseline is
flat everywhere, expansion is a necessary condition for the decline, and
we do not claim every expanding pipeline declines in every setting.

These verdicts come from a single judging date and are not pooled with
the earlier batches (\S\ref{sec:c4}); the GPT-5.4 leg, on which the
original corpus-conditional claim also rested, has not been re-run at
this power and remains the one open thread.

\subsection{C4: Judges Are Unstable on Frozen Inputs and on Changed Ones}
\label{sec:c4}
\looseness=-1
Table~\ref{tab:judges} separates judge instability into a component
measured on \emph{frozen} inputs, where no retrieval is re-run, and one
measured under a changed retrieval state.
\noindent\textit{Frozen inputs.} Re-judging the identical 300
(answer, context) pairs -- the stored strings, byte for byte -- gives
GPT-5.4 $\kappa{=}0.76$ (raw agreement 0.88) on the same day, but only
$\kappa{=}0.14$ (0.56) eleven weeks later, a $+15$\,pp leniency shift.
Sampling noise cannot explain the gap: under a stationary judge,
cross-date agreement should sit at the same-day level, and the observed
0.56 rejects stationarity (binomial $p{<}10^{-44}$). GPT-4.1 on the
same frozen tuples is starker: $\kappa{=}{-}0.05/{-}0.00$ (v2/v3),
chance-level agreement with its own earlier verdicts, a $+41$\,pp
leniency shift. With the inputs held fixed, this component is judge
drift alone.
\noindent\textit{Changed retrieval state.} Re-judging after an embedder
swap -- which changes retrieved contexts and answers on 94\% of tuples
-- gives GPT-5.4 $\kappa{=}0.137$ (41\% of verdicts flip) against the
$0.76$ same-day floor; GPT-4.1 flips only 26\% ($\kappa{=}0.48$). This
component mixes output drift with judge response, and the two judges
rank oppositely on the two axes: the judge more stable under embedder
swap is the less stable across time, so stability on one axis does not
transfer to the other.
\noindent\textit{Across vendors, same day.} Adding a third vendor
(Gemini 3.7-flash, thinking enabled) as a judge on identical rows on a
single date sharpens the picture. Gemini is uniformly the more lenient
judge -- 0.98--0.99 faithful against GPT-4.1's 0.92--0.95 -- and
McNemar rejects symmetric disagreement everywhere
($p{<}10^{-9}$), so the gap is systematic, not noise. The two agree on
91--94\% of rows, yet Cohen's $\kappa$ reads $0.03$--$0.13$ while
Gwet's AC1 reads $0.90$--$0.94$ (Table~\ref{tab:judges}, bottom). This
is the prevalence paradox of Feinstein and Cicchetti~\cite{feinstein1990}
in a cross-vendor pair: at 93\% agreement, reporting $\kappa$ alone
would license the claim that two judges agree no better than chance.
Crucially, leniency and \emph{trend} come apart --- despite sitting near
the ceiling, Gemini still reproduces the hop-wise decline
($p{=}4.3{\times}10^{-3}$ and $p{<}10^{-16}$ on the two main matrices).
A judge's strictness and the ordering it induces are separable
properties, and only the first is vendor-specific.

\noindent\textit{Between judges.} Inter-judge $\kappa$ halves under
embedder swap on identical tuples (0.30 $\to$ 0.17 overall); McNemar
rejects symmetric disagreement (exact $p{<}0.001$; GPT-4.1
systematically more lenient). On MuSiQue the pair agrees at chance
level ($\kappa{=}0.02$, raw agreement 0.48, Gwet's AC1 0.08 -- genuine
disagreement, not a prevalence artifact; GPT-4.1 judges 88\% faithful
vs.\ GPT-5.4's 47\%), so C3's cross-corpus statements are made per
judge, each independently showing no collapse. The replication batches
(C3) show what drift does at scale: eleven weeks on, GPT-4.1 marks
93--95\% of fresh same-distribution tuples faithful, inter-judge
$\kappa$ falls to $0.05/0.01$ (local/Azure), and the trend GPT-4.1
itself had established in the original batches disappears.

\paragraph{C5 (supporting): Dense embeddings alone classify hop.}
Table~\ref{tab:router} shows the V2 router replication. The 50-fold
macro-$F_1$ improves $0.78\pm0.08\to 0.86\pm0.05$ under embedder swap,
and Adaptive-V2 closes 59\%/55\% of the V1$\to$Oracle gap
(local/Azure), with Holm-significant per-stratum gains in 3/3 strata
locally and 2/3 under Azure (the 3+-hop routing targets are
statistically tied there). Only 4 of the 11 hand text
features are ever non-zero on the 296 queries; the rest are inert. The router therefore operates almost
exclusively on the 3 query-embedding PCs, suggesting hop distance is
largely an embedding-decodable property of the query text in our
generated query set; transfer to human-authored queries is untested.
