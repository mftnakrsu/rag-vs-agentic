\subsection{C1: Winners are Corpus-Conditional, Embedder-Robust}
\label{sec:c1}
Table~\ref{tab:dominance} reports per-stratum $F_1$ across three settings.
On DO-178C the dominance pattern is identical under both embedders:
agentic-graph wins 1-hop and 3+-hop, vanilla wins 2-hop. The 1-hop and
3+-hop wins do not separate agentic-graph from agentic (Cliff's
$\delta{<}0.147$ in every stratum) and are read as an \{agentic-graph,
agentic\} tie. On MuSiQue (three-pipeline field: vanilla, agentic-graph, GraphRAG) the
winners shift: agentic-graph retains 1-hop, but GraphRAG wins both 2-hop
($F_1{=}0.375$) and 3+-hop ($F_1{=}0.342$), reversing vanilla's 2-hop
dominance.

\subsection{C2: GraphRAG's Universal Pathology, Conditional Consequence}
\label{sec:c2}
\noindent\textbf{(C2a) Over-citation is setting-universal}
(Table~\ref{tab:pathology}, top).
Across embedder \emph{and} corpus swaps GraphRAG cites a mean of 11--15
IDs per answer at citation precision 0.12--0.23 with retrieval recall
in the 0.68--0.87 range. Against the context cap of 15, a mean of
14.9--15.0 cited IDs on DO-178C means the synthesizer cites essentially
every context supplied.

\noindent\textbf{(C2b) The faithfulness consequence is corpus-conditional.}
The bottom block reveals the consequence split. On DO-178C, GraphRAG
faithfulness declines with hop distance: Cochran--Armitage trend
$p{=}0.040/0.008$ (GPT-5.4/GPT-4.1, local embedder) and $p{<}10^{-4}$
(GPT-4.1, Azure); only GPT-5.4-under-Azure is flat ($p{=}0.51$). On
MuSiQue neither judge collapses: GPT-5.4 \emph{rises} (n.s.,
$p{=}0.068$) while GPT-4.1 declines mildly from a 94\% base to 80\%
($p{=}0.017$) --- both 3+-hop endpoints sit far above the DO-178C
endpoints (58\%/80\% vs.\ 40\%/33\%). Over-citation is
therefore setting-universal but consequentially domain-bound ---
a constraint on inferring faithfulness from citation precision alone;
we hypothesize the mechanism in \S\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{C3: Single-Judge Faithfulness is Retrieval-State-Fragile}
\label{sec:c3}
Table~\ref{tab:judges} reports inter-judge agreement on the paired
300-tuple subset and three same-judge controls. Inter-judge $\kappa$
between GPT-5.4 and GPT-4.1 halves under embedder swap on identical
tuples (0.30 $\to$ 0.17 overall); McNemar rejects symmetric disagreement
(exact $p{<}0.001$; GPT-4.1 systematically more lenient). On MuSiQue
the same pair agrees at chance level ($\kappa{=}0.02$, raw agreement
0.48; GPT-4.1 judges 88\% faithful vs.\ GPT-5.4's 47\%): C2b's
cross-corpus statements are per judge, each independently showing no
collapse. The headline is the joint read of the same-judge block: same-input same-day
test--retest gives $\kappa{=}0.76$ (12\% of verdicts flip on pure
resampling), while re-judging the same tuples after an embedder swap
gives $\kappa{=}0.14$ (41\% flip). The swap changes retrieved contexts
and answers on 94\% of tuples: the gap thus measures verdict
sensitivity to \emph{upstream retrieval state} (output drift and judge
response jointly) against a known noise floor. A judge-specific
component remains: GPT-4.1 flips only 26\% on the same items
($\kappa{=}0.48$). A final control: the same deployment re-judging the
identical 300 inputs eleven weeks later agrees with its own earlier
verdicts at only $\kappa{=}0.14$ ($+15$\,pp leniency shift).

\paragraph{C4 (supporting): Dense embeddings alone classify hop.}
Table~\ref{tab:router} shows the V2 router replication. The 50-fold
macro-$F_1$ improves $0.78\pm0.08\to 0.86\pm0.05$ under embedder swap,
and Adaptive-V2 closes 56\% of the V1$\to$Oracle gap with Holm-significant
gains in all three strata under both embedders. Only 4 of the 11 hand text
features are ever non-zero on the 296 queries; the rest are inert. The router therefore operates almost
exclusively on the 3 query-embedding PCs, suggesting hop distance is
largely an embedding-decodable property of the query text in our
generated query set; transfer to human-authored queries is untested.
