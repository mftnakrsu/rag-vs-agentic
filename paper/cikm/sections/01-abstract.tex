\begin{abstract}
Reported verdicts on GraphRAG versus vector RAG disagree, and the
evidence is typically tied to a single corpus, embedder, and judge --
and, we show, to where citation quality is measured. We present a
triple-robustness analysis that holds a five-pipeline architecture
matrix fixed and varies embedder (local e5-small vs.\ Azure
text-embedding-3-small), corpus (DO-178C typed-edge requirements vs.\
Wikipedia paragraph chains via MuSiQue), and judge (paired GPT-5.4
$\times$ GPT-4.1 on both corpora), over $2{\times}4{,}440$ main-matrix
runs at three seeds, 600 cross-corpus runs, and over 5{,}000
faithfulness judgments. Our central finding concerns the measurement
point rather than the architecture: GraphRAG's graph walk floods the
context window at precision 0.12--0.23, while the synthesizer it feeds
cites selectively at precision 0.48--0.65. Scoring the retrieved set as
the attribution set therefore ranks GraphRAG last and scoring the
answer's citations ranks it first or tied-first, which reconciles part
of the disagreement in prior reports. Downstream of that flooding,
faithfulness is corpus-conditional: it declines with hop distance on
DO-178C (trend $p{<}0.05$ in three of four judge$\times$embedder
combinations) but shows no collapse on Wikipedia chains under either
judge. Answer-level citation winners are likewise corpus- and
stratum-conditional, yet stable under embedder swap and across seeds.
Faithfulness verdicts are not stable: re-judging frozen
answer-context pairs eleven weeks apart gives self-agreement
$\kappa{\leq}0.14$ for both judges, against a same-day test--retest
floor of $0.76$. We argue that RAG architecture claims should be tested
at this level of robustness -- including robustness to the
citation-measurement point -- before they are trusted.
\end{abstract}
