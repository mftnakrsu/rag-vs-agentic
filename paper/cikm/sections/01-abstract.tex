\begin{abstract}
Reported verdicts on GraphRAG versus vector RAG disagree, and the
evidence is typically tied to a single corpus, embedder, and judge --
and, we show, to where citation quality is measured. We vary all four:
a five-pipeline architecture matrix held fixed while we swap the
embedder (e5-small 384d, text-embedding-3-small 1536d, bge-m3 1024d),
the corpus (DO-178C typed-edge requirements, and Wikipedia paragraph
chains via MuSiQue and 2WikiMultihopQA), the graph traversal (bounded
2-hop walk vs.\ personalized PageRank), and the judge (GPT-5.4, GPT-4.1
and Gemini 3.7-flash), over 10{,}656 generation runs at three seeds and
more than 24{,}000 faithfulness judgments. Our central finding concerns
the measurement point rather than the architecture: GraphRAG's graph
walk floods the context window at precision 0.12--0.23, while the
synthesizer it feeds cites selectively at 0.48--0.98. Scoring the
retrieved set as the attribution set therefore ranks GraphRAG last and
scoring the answer's citations ranks it first or tied-first, in all six
settings we tested; the split survives a change of traversal and defeats
two obvious mitigations, since the synthesizer already performs the
filtering. Answer-level winners are stratum-conditional but stable
across embedders and seeds. Faithfulness declines with hop distance on
\emph{both} corpus families once the comparison is adequately powered --
a corpus-conditional reading we previously reported does not survive --
while the one pipeline that expands no context never declines. Judges,
by contrast, are not stable: re-judging frozen answer--context pairs
eleven weeks later gives self-agreement $\kappa{\leq}0.14$, and two
vendors judging identical rows on one day agree on 93\% of them at
$\kappa{=}0.03$--$0.13$ yet AC1 $0.90$--$0.94$. We argue that RAG
architecture claims should be tested at this level of robustness --
including robustness to the citation-measurement point -- before they
are trusted.
\end{abstract}
